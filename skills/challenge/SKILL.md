# Challenge

Attack the weakest parts of a candidate conclusion before adjudication.

## Process

### 1. Extract load-bearing claims

Identify the claims that the final conclusion depends on.

**Completion criterion:** weakening any extracted claim would materially change the conclusion.

### 2. Seek disconfirmation

For each claim, look for:
- direct counterevidence;
- failed replications or conflicting observations;
- boundary conditions;
- base-rate neglect;
- alternative causal explanations;
- reversed causality or confounding;
- cases the claim cannot explain.

**Completion criterion:** every load-bearing claim has been actively challenged rather than only supported.

### 3. Check source independence

Trace apparently different sources to see whether they depend on the same underlying study, dataset, institution, or claim chain.

**Completion criterion:** duplicated evidence is not counted as independent corroboration.

### 4. Check scope

Compare what the evidence directly supports with what the claim asserts:
- population;
- context;
- time period;
- intervention / exposure;
- outcome;
- causal strength.

**Completion criterion:** no claim silently exceeds its evidence scope.

### 5. Return the stress test

Mark each challenge as:
- survives;
- weakened;
- contradicted;
- unresolved.

## Boundary

Challenge does not produce the final verdict. Its job is to make hidden fragility visible for later adjudication.
